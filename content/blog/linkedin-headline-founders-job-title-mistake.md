## Step 1: Understand What a LinkedIn Headline Actually Does

Your LinkedIn headline is a short, keyword-rich field, up to 220 characters, sitting directly beneath your name on every search result, comment, and connection request you appear in. It is the highest-weighted field in LinkedIn's search algorithm, meaning keywords placed there carry more ranking power than anywhere else on your profile, including your work history. Most advice stops there and tells you to [fill it with keywords so recruiters can find you](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/impactful-linkedin-headline-examples/).

That is the right answer to the wrong question if you are a founder or executive.

A headline optimised for recruiter search does exactly what it is designed to do: it surfaces your profile to people hiring for a role. The failure mode unique to founders and senior professionals is not a poorly written headline. It is a technically correct headline that draws recruiting coordinators and job boards while suppressing the prospects, partners, and inbound deal conversations that actually move the business. Fixing the keyword density does not fix that problem. Choosing the right goal before you write a single word does.

## Step 2: Choose What Job Your Headline Is Doing

Before you touch the headline field, ask yourself one question: who do I need to recognize my name and click through? The answer determines every word that follows. A founder building inbound pipeline and a senior professional open to new roles are optimising for entirely different readers, and a headline that serves both simultaneously serves neither well. You can [see what a strong LinkedIn profile delivers](https://www.withsignalhouse.com/case-studies) when that distinction is made clearly from the start.

### If you are a founder or executive building inbound pipeline

Your reader is a potential client, a partner, or someone who was referred to you and is now checking whether you are worth a conversation. They are not searching LinkedIn's recruiter filters. They are skimming your profile in thirty seconds, often on a phone, deciding whether the problem you solve matches the problem they have. Your headline needs to name who you work with and what changes for them, not what your job title is.

### If you are a senior professional open to new roles

Here the recruiter search logic applies. Your reader is using LinkedIn's talent tools, filtering by title, function, and industry. Keywords matched to the roles you want carry real weight, and the headline should reflect the position you are moving toward, not just the one you hold. This is the narrower use case for the keyword-density approach, and it is the one almost every competing article is actually written for.

Most founders sit in the first category and write a headline for the second. That is the whole problem.

## Step 3: Build Your Headline Using a Formula That Fits Your Goal

LinkedIn's headline has three functions: it helps you get found via search, it signals something specific about what you do, and it [brands your profile so you are not treated as a commodity](https://www.forbes.com/sites/williamarruda/2025/08/14/how-to-craft-the-perfect-linkedin-headline-with-examples/). A formula only does all three if it is matched to the right goal. There is no single template that works across both cases.

The first 80 characters carry the most weight: that is what appears in mobile search results and preview cards. Whatever matters most goes there first, not after the second pipe character.

**For founders and executives targeting clients or partners, use a value-outcome formula:**

- **Who you help:** Name the audience in terms they use for themselves. "B2B SaaS founders" lands better than "technology companies."
- **What changes for them:** State the outcome, not the service. "Close enterprise deals faster" says more than "sales consulting."
- **Your credibility marker:** One short phrase that earns the claim, placed after the core statement where truncation risk is lower.

An example of what this looks like in practice: "I help B2B SaaS founders build sales pipelines that close | Ex-Salesforce, 3x operator." The claim is front-loaded. The credential follows. A prospect skimming on a phone gets the essential point inside the preview window.

**For senior professionals open to roles, use a title-skills-niche formula:**

- **Target title:** The exact phrase a recruiter would type into a search filter.
- **Core skill set:** Two or three terms that match the job descriptions you want.
- **Industry or niche:** Narrows the match and reduces irrelevant outreach.

An example: "Head of Growth | Demand Generation, Paid Acquisition | B2B SaaS." Clean, scannable, and built around the terms that appear in the roles you are targeting.

## Step 4: What to Avoid in a LinkedIn Headline

These are the mistakes we see most often on founder and executive profiles, and for each one the real cost is not aesthetic. It is who contacts you, or does not.

- **Leaving LinkedIn's auto-generated title:** LinkedIn defaults to your current job title and company name. That tells a reader your employment status. It tells them nothing about why they should reach out. Prospects move on because there is no signal that you solve anything they care about.
- **Burying the value claim after the third separator:** On mobile, the headline truncates early. If your strongest phrase sits after two pipe characters, most of your audience never reads it. The prospect sees your title, not your offer.
- **Using insider jargon:** Terms that read as expertise to a peer in your field read as noise to the buyer one level up or one industry over. If your prospect has to translate your headline, they do not. They keep scrolling.
- **Listing traits instead of outcomes:** "Passionate about helping companies grow" says nothing a prospect can act on. It signals that you have not thought carefully about who you are talking to. Replace any "passionate about" construction with a specific outcome the reader actually wants.
- **Treating it as a one-time task:** A headline written for a launch-stage offer is wrong the moment you move upmarket. The field should reflect the current offer and the current audience, not the version of the business from eighteen months ago.

## Step 5: Review and Refine Your Headline Before You Publish

Before you publish, run your draft through three questions. They take sixty seconds and catch the problems that rewrites miss.

Does it name who you serve? Not in a category so broad it covers everyone, but specifically enough that the right person reading it thinks "that is me." Does it state what changes for them? A title describes you. An outcome describes why someone should care. Does it make the reader curious enough to click the profile? If the headline already answers every question, there is no reason to open the page.

On timing: revisit the headline when your content strategy shifts, when your offer changes, or when the inbound you are getting no longer matches the inbound you want. Not on a quarterly calendar. Not as part of a scheduled profile audit. The trigger is a change in what you are selling or who you are selling it to. That is the operational signal, and it is the one generic advice never mentions because it requires knowing how the work actually runs day to day.

## FAQ

### How long should a LinkedIn headline be?

LinkedIn allows up to 220 characters. For founders and executives targeting inbound from prospects or partners, aim to put your core value statement in the first 80 characters, since that is what appears on mobile previews. The remaining space works well for a credibility marker or niche qualifier, not filler.

### What is an example of a good LinkedIn headline for a founder?

A founder targeting B2B clients might use: "I help mid-market SaaS companies reduce churn through onboarding design | Founder, 10 years in customer success." The outcome comes first. The credential follows. A recruiter sees a specialist; a prospect sees someone who solves a problem they recognize.

### Should I put my company name in my LinkedIn headline?

Only if the company name itself carries recognition with your target audience. For most founders, the company name takes up character space without adding context for a prospect who has never heard of it. Use that space for the outcome you deliver instead. The company name is already visible one line below in your experience section.

## Start with the Goal, Not the Formula

Every guide on how to write a LinkedIn headline jumps straight to templates. The reason most founder headlines still do not work is not that the template was wrong. It is that no one asked what the headline was supposed to accomplish before filling it in.

Choose your audience first. Build the formula second. Put the most important phrase in the first 80 characters. Then leave it alone until something real changes.
